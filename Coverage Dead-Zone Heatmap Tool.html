    The problem

    Found too late

    Dead zones surface only after users complain or after a costly drive test.

    Hard to read

    Raw dBm tables are slow to compare, and paid tools like Atoll and Planet are complex.

    No way out

    Someone stuck in a dead zone cannot tell where to find signal.

    Telecom planners, rural and hilly communities, emergency services, schools and small ISPs all feel the gap: dropped calls and no emergency access.

    How it works

    1. Enter
    Location, power, frequency, antenna height, environment
    2. Validate
    Range checks and unit conversion
    3. Calculate
    Path loss for every grid cell, plus terrain line-of-sight
    4. Classify
    Five zones from Strong to Dead using adjustable thresholds
    5. Show
    Colour heatmap, coverage % and dead-zone list

    Models and zones

    ModelUse
    Free-spaceClear line of sight, best-case baseline
    Log-distanceExponent set by environment: urban 3.5, suburban 3.0, rural 2.5
    COST-231 HataUrban, suburban and rural macro cells, with environment corrections

    Default zone limits: Strong above −85 dBm, Fair above −95, Weak above −105, Poor above −115, Dead below.
